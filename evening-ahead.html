A Note Before You Arrive

You have been extended an invitation that most people will never receive. What follows is not a list of rules. It is an orientation into an experience designed with extraordinary care, for your pleasure, for your marriage, and for the charge of the room you are about to share with a small group of remarkable couples.

You are not attending a party. You are choosing an erotically alive marriage.

Everything that follows exists for one reason: so that when you walk through those doors, nothing surprises you except how deeply you feel it.

Practical Matters

  • Eat a real meal before you arrive. In the deep evening there will be light nourishment, but dinner is yours to handle before you walk in. Come nourished.
  • Avoid garlic, onion, and anything that will follow you into the room. This is a sensory environment and you will be close to people. Breath matters. This is an act of respect for yourself and everyone around you.
  • Your arrival window is confirmed and fixed. It was designed so that each couple has an arrival experience. Arrive within it. Doors close at 9:15pm. No one will be available to let you in after that.
  • For attire, we have curated a moodboard that shows exactly the tone we are reaching for. This is not a costume party and it is not resort casual. Dress as though someone extraordinary might be watching, because they will be.
  • Come when you are solid. This weekend amplifies what is already between you. It does not repair what is strained. If the timing is not right, tell us.

The way you arrive matters, not as a performance, but as a gesture toward your own marriage and the other couples in the room.

The Language of the Room

Your masks are not costumes. They are a language, the most important one spoken in this room. Learn it before you arrive. Everything that follows depends on it.

Masks are one of the few things we ask of every couple on arrival, and the reason matters. When everyone arrives masked, no one is singled out. The couple choosing copper, who simply wants to witness the evening, is not marked as different from anyone else in the room. The mask creates equality of presence from the moment you walk in.

This couple is present to witness, to feel the room, to exist inside the atmosphere together. They are not available for approach, conversation, or engagement of any kind. Masks can be changed at any moment, in either direction, with no explanation owed to anyone.

The Open Field

Living Art

Once the room opens, you may find yourself in a space with other couples, or they may find themselves in a space with you. This is the nature of a shared erotic field. What it requires is one understanding above all others:

If you choose to be seen, you are, in that moment, living art. Beautiful. Sovereign. Completely yours, yet the room does not belong to you.

The spaces belong to Voluptas, and any couple choosing to express themselves are consenting to becoming beautiful live erotic art. They are there to be visually consumed. You don't need permission to witness.

What you don't do is interrupt. A couple in the middle of something is in their own world. You don't tap a shoulder, you don't hover waiting for eye contact, you don't ask. You either settle nearby and let your presence speak, or you move on.

If a couch or shared space is open in a room, it is yours. A bed is a different situation. The bed itself is sacred space. You cannot invite yourself onto it. If a couple wants company, they will make that known. The invitation comes from them, not from you.

Parallel expression near another couple, each couple entirely in their own world, proximity creating charge without contact, is one of the most natural things in this room. It requires nothing except reading the space correctly.

When a couple in a space turns toward you, you will know. Trust that. If an acknowledgment comes, follow it or don't. But do not manufacture it. Desire moves freely when no one feels cornered.

Your Responsibility to the Room

What You Bring

You are not an audience. You are part of the atmosphere. Every couple in this room contributes to the charge of the evening or draws from it. Come with curiosity, warmth, and presence.

  • Move at the pace of the slower partner. Always. The person who is less ready sets the speed, not as a constraint, but as an act of devotion. The most erotic thing you can do for each other tonight is refuse to rush.
  • If something feels heavy between you, take it to the private space near the entry, or step outside. Resolve it. Return when you are solid. The erotic field amplifies what is already between you. Come to it whole.
  • Nothing is expected. A completely valid and deeply beautiful Voluptas evening is one where you feel the room, feel each other, and return to your suite having touched no one outside your marriage. That is the product working exactly as it was designed.

  • How to decline gracefully: a smile and returning your attention to your partner is a complete sentence. You owe no one an explanation for any boundary at any time.
  • Do not announce your intentions or your limits unprompted. Nobody needs to know what you plan to do or not do tonight. Let the evening unfold.
  • When you are ready to leave, you leave. There is no checkout, no farewell lap, no announcement. Collect your phone, step out, and carry the night home. The Irish goodbye was invented for a room exactly like this one.
